# V1.4 review: Bellman–Ford and negative-weight paths

## Learning sequence

The permanent `bellman-ford` lesson follows Weighted Graphs and Shortest Paths in Algorithms, with Graphs, Big O, and the weighted lesson as advisory prerequisites. It contrasts Dijkstra's nonnegative settled-distance invariant with a genuine directed counterexample: A → B (2), A → C (5), C → B (−10). A negative edge can break Dijkstra's guarantee without forcing an incorrect result in every graph. Worked passes, Python, misconceptions, and three knowledge checks cover signed path sums, strict relaxation, the V−1 simple-path bound, unchanged-pass early stop, and an additional reachable-improvement check. The lesson distinguishes finite, unreachable, and negatively unbounded destinations and compares BFS, Dijkstra, and Bellman–Ford.

## Graph engine and trace invariants

`bellman-ford-steps.ts` accepts 1–9 distinct A–I vertices, at most 18 directed edges, and safe integer weights −20 through 20. It rejects duplicate directed pairs, unknown endpoints, invalid source or destination, invalid labels, and malformed weights. Zero-weight edges, self-loops, reciprocal arcs, and isolates are legal. An undirected negative edge traversable both ways would produce a negative closed walk; the V1.4 composer is directed. Edge scans sort by source and target label. Only strict improvements change a distance and predecessor, so equal costs retain the first parent. The source begins at zero; other vertices begin at infinity.

Each complete pass scans every edge, with no early stop until a full pass has no updates. At most V−1 passes cover simple finite routes. The extra check considers only edges whose source is reachable. Relaxable targets seed directed downstream propagation; affected vertices have no finite shortest-path cost, while reachable unaffected branches retain their finite answers. Temporary estimates are labeled provisional in the player. Final paths are reconstructed only for finite destinations, with unknown-node and cycle guards. A displayed cycle witness is checked against actual directed edges and a negative total; detection remains truthful when no verified witness can be extracted. The player stores frozen snapshots for initialization, pass starts, inspections, relaxations, summaries, early stopping, detection, propagation, and completion. Snapshot storage is teaching overhead beyond Bellman–Ford's O(VE) time and O(V) algorithmic auxiliary space.
